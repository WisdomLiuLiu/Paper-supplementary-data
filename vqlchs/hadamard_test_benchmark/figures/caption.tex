\textbf{Gate-count reduction and overlap accuracy across system sizes.}
(a) Single-qubit (1q) and two-qubit (2q) gate counts before (solid lines)
and after (dashed lines) the shared-structure simplification with the reverse
construction for the propagation block. The circuits are compiled into
single-qubit $U$ gates and two-qubit CX gates on a linear chain of $n$ system
qubits and one ancillary qubit, including routing overhead.
(b) Absolute complex-overlap deviation
$\epsilon_X=\max_{s\in\mathcal S}|X_{\mathrm{after}}(s)-X_{\mathrm{before}}(s)|$
versus $n$, where $\mathcal S$ contains the two tested computational-basis
input states before PQC preparation.
Both panels use one second-order Trotter step for the open-boundary HN model
with $g=U=1$, $k=1$, $\delta t=0.2$, and two five-layer PQCs with fixed random
parameters. The original and simplified circuits use the same ordered
symmetric product formula. These benchmarks show substantial gate-count
reductions while retaining small overlap deviations across the tested sizes.
The deviations characterize these tested state pairs rather than a
worst-case operator error.
